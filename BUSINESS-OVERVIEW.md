# 3% Club Dashboard — What It Is and What It Does

A complete guide for management. No technical knowledge needed.

---

## 1. In one paragraph

Today your customer conversations live on WhatsApp, and everything you know
about a customer lives in someone's head or someone's phone. This system puts
every customer, every conversation, and every commitment in one place — and
handles the repetitive part of the conversation automatically, so your staff
spend their time only on the people who actually need a person.

---

## 2. The problem it solves

Here is what typically happens today, and what changes.

| Today | With the system |
|---|---|
| A customer messages. Whoever sees it first replies. | Every message lands in one shared inbox, with that customer's full history beside it. |
| Nobody remembers where a customer came from. | Every customer carries their source — which campaign, which agent, which referral — permanently. |
| "Did anyone follow up with him?" | Every follow-up has an owner, a due date, and a visible outcome. |
| Introducing the club is retyped every time, differently. | The introduction runs automatically, worded the same way every time. |
| Someone says "not interested" and is either forgotten or pestered. | The system waits exactly one day, asks once more, and then stops. Permanently. |
| Staff performance is a feeling. | Staff performance is a number built from recorded facts. |
| "Which campaign actually worked?" | Every campaign shows audience, sent, delivered, read, replies, and conversions. |

**The core shift:** the machine handles the predictable conversation. Your
people handle the human one. Right now your people are doing both, and the
predictable part is eating the time that should go to closing.

---

## 3. How it works, start to finish

This is the full journey of one customer.

**Step 1 — A customer enters.**
They come from a WhatsApp campaign, your website, Facebook, Instagram, a
referral, an agent, a dealer, or manual entry by staff. Whichever it was, it is
recorded and never overwritten.

**Step 2 — The introduction goes out automatically.**
The system sends a short message introducing the 3% Club and asks one question:
*Would you like to know more?* The customer answers YES or NO.

**Step 3a — If they say YES.**
The system marks them Interested, then sends the benefits, then the key
information, then the common questions. Finally it asks whether they would like
a representative to contact them.

If they say yes to that, three things happen at once: a call request is created,
a follow-up is scheduled, and the responsible staff member is notified. The
customer is now a human's job, and the system stops talking.

**Step 3b — If they say NO.**
This is the part most systems get wrong, and it is worth reading carefully.

- The NO is recorded — the question, the answer, the time.
- The information journey **stops immediately**. No more benefit messages.
- The customer is marked for revision.
- The system waits **exactly one day**. Not "about a day".
- After that day it sends one revision message and asks once more.
- If they now say YES, they rejoin at the benefits step — they are *not* sent
  the introduction again.
- If they say NO a second time, it is recorded, the journey stops, and the
  system will **never send that message again**.

The customer stays in your database for future business. They are simply left
alone.

**Step 3c — If they say nothing at all.**
The system waits a configured period, sends one gentle follow-up, and then
stops. It does not chase.

**Step 4 — A human takes over.**
Staff see the customer with everything already in front of them: where they came
from, every message, every answer, their interest level, their pipeline stage,
and what the next action is.

**Step 5 — The lead moves through the pipeline.**
Ten stages: New → Contacted → Interested → Follow-up → Qualified → Call
Requested → Meeting Requested → Negotiation → Converted → Lost. Every single
stage change is recorded with who moved it and when.

**Step 6 — Everything becomes a number.**
How many came in, from where, how many said yes, how many converted, who is
handling the most work, which campaign paid off.

## 4. What is inside — the fourteen screens

| Screen | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | The morning view. Total customers, new leads, interested, follow-ups due, calls, meetings — and today's overdue work. |
| **WhatsApp Inbox** | Every conversation in one place. Reply, send an approved template, change status, assign to someone, close the conversation. The customer's full context sits beside the chat. |
| **Customers** | Search and filter everyone. Name, phone, source, campaign, owner, stage, interest, next follow-up, last contact. |
| **Customer profile** | One customer's complete story on a single timeline — every message, answer, stage change, call, meeting, follow-up and automated action, in order. |
| **Leads** | The ten-stage pipeline with a live count at each stage. |
| **Follow-ups** | Today, Upcoming, Overdue, Completed — plus call requests and meeting requests. |
| **Campaigns** | Build an audience, pick a message, schedule it, watch the results. |
| **Automations** | The library of automatic journeys, their controls, and what is scheduled to happen next. |
| **Templates** | Reusable messages, organised by category, with a count of where each is used. |
| **FAQ** | Standard answers to common questions, used inside the automatic journeys so everyone gets the same answer. |
| **Staff** | Who is carrying how much work, and how much of it is overdue. |
| **Staff Scoring** | Performance measurement (see section 7 — this needs a decision from you). |
| **Analytics** | Every number, filterable by date, source, campaign, staff, and stage. |
| **Activity** | A permanent record of everything that happened and who did it. |
| **Settings** | Emergency controls and the business decisions the system is waiting on. |

## 5. Who can do what

Four roles. Each person sees only what their role allows — and this is enforced
by the system, not by trust.

| Role | What they can do |
|---|---|
| **Admin** | Everything, including the emergency controls. |
| **Manager** | Customers, conversations, leads, campaigns, automations, templates, reports, staff performance. Cannot use emergency controls or change permissions. |
| **CRM / Sales Staff** | Only the customers assigned to them. They can reply, update leads, manage follow-ups, and raise call and meeting requests. They cannot see other people's customers, and cannot create campaigns or change automations. |
| **Viewer** | Read-only. Can look, cannot change anything. |

**Important:** staff see only their own assigned customers. This was a
deliberate choice — the source requirements describe staff working their own
book, and it was safer to restrict than to assume. If you want staff to see the
whole database, that is a decision you can make and it can be changed.

## 6. The safety controls

This system sends messages to real customers. That is the thing most worth
getting wrong, so it has specific guardrails.

**Pause everything.** One button stops every automatic journey instantly.
Nothing is lost — conversations keep their place and resume exactly where they
stopped when you switch it back on. Admin only, and every use is recorded.

**Stop one campaign.** A running campaign can be stopped mid-send. It stops
immediately, not at the end of the batch, and it tells you how many people were
never messaged. A stopped campaign cannot be restarted — because restarting
would message people you deliberately cut off.

**Stop one customer's journey.** If one customer should be left alone, their
journey can be stopped individually, with the reason recorded.

**No accidental double-sends.** If the messaging network hiccups and sends the
same event twice, the system recognises it and acts once. The same protection
applies if a job restarts halfway through.

**Respecting a NO.** Covered in section 3b. This is built into the structure of
the journey, not a rule someone has to remember.

**A complete record.** Every meaningful action is logged permanently — who did
it, when, and what changed. The log cannot be edited or deleted from inside the
product. That is the point of it.

## 7. What management gets to see

**On the dashboard, immediately:** how many customers, how many new leads, how
many are interested, what is due today, what is overdue, and recent activity.

**In analytics, filtered any way you like:** customers by source and campaign,
messages sent and delivered and read, replies, how many said YES and how many
said NO, revision responses, leads at each stage, calls, meetings, conversions,
campaign performance, and staff workload.

**The source performance view** answers the question that matters most for
spending: for each source — Facebook, Instagram, referrals, agents — how many
customers came in, how many became interested, how many asked for a call, how
many met, how many converted. That tells you where your money is actually
working.

**Every number explains itself.** Each figure can show you exactly what it
counts and over what time period. There are no numbers you cannot trace back.

## 8. Where the system deliberately says "not defined"

You will see the phrase **"Not defined"** in a few places. This is intentional
and worth understanding, because it is a feature, not a gap.

The documentation your system was built from left certain business decisions
open — thirty-five of them. Rather than guess a value and let it quietly become
your company policy, the system shows "Not defined" and refuses to act on it.

An example: staff scoring. The system measures twelve things about each staff
member — leads handled, customers contacted, follow-ups completed, follow-ups
completed on time, overdue work, calls, meetings, qualified leads, conversions,
and inactivity. All of that is real and visible today.

But it will **not** produce an overall score, because nobody has decided what
each factor is worth. If it simply added the numbers up, the person with the
biggest workload would always win — which measures workload, not performance.
Since people's appraisals may depend on that number, the system would rather
show you the honest inputs than a confident wrong answer.

The moment you approve the weights, the score appears — and it shows its own
arithmetic, so anyone can see how it was reached.

## 9. What is built, and what is not

Being straight with you: **this is not ready to go live yet.** Most of it is
built. What is missing is mostly decisions from you and operational setup, not
software.

**Built and working:**

- Customer management with complete history
- The shared WhatsApp inbox
- The full 3% Club journey including the NO → one day → revision rule
- The automation engine with all its safety controls
- Lead pipeline and follow-ups
- Call and meeting handling
- Campaigns with audience building and result tracking
- Templates and FAQ
- Analytics and source performance
- Staff workload and the scoring framework
- Activity log and settings
- Login, four roles, and permission enforcement

**Not ready:**

- **No live WhatsApp connection yet.** The system is built to work with a
  WhatsApp provider, but no provider has been chosen. Until then, no real
  message can be sent.
- **The scheduler is not running.** The part that wakes up and says "this
  customer's day is up, send the revision message" exists, but nothing is
  calling it yet. **Until this is switched on, no timed action happens at all.**
  This is a half-hour setup job, not a build job.
- **No automated safety net yet.** There are seventy-one automated checks, but
  they test the logic in isolation, not against a live database. Full end-to-end
  testing is designed but not built.
- **No backups tested, no monitoring, no alerts.**

## 10. What we need from you

These are the decisions only the business can make. The two marked **blocking**
prevent launch entirely.

| # | Decision | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Which WhatsApp provider** — *blocking* | Nothing can be sent without it. This is a commercial choice with a cost. |
| 2 | **How long to wait before the no-response follow-up** — *blocking* | The main journey cannot be switched on until this number exists. Something like 24 or 48 hours. |
| 3 | Staff scoring weights | Until decided, no performance score is produced. |
| 4 | Which words mean "stop messaging me" | Until decided, a customer replying STOP is treated as an ordinary reply. |
| 5 | Your business timezone for reports | Until decided, daily figures use the server's day, not yours. |
| 6 | When a follow-up becomes urgent | Until decided, overdue work is visible but nothing escalates. |
| 7 | The list of customer statuses | Until decided, that field stays free text. |
| 8 | The list of meeting statuses | Same. |
| 9 | The list of outcomes | Same — and scoring can only count that an outcome exists, not what it was worth. |
| 10 | Should staff see customers not assigned to them | Currently they cannot. Safer default, easily changed. |

Decisions 2 through 9 are entered directly in the Settings screen, which shows
each one alongside exactly what it costs to leave it open. No developer is
needed to change them.

## 11. What this is worth

**Time saved.** The introduction, the benefits, the FAQ and the first follow-up
happen without anyone typing. For a hundred new customers a month, that is
several hundred messages your staff never write.

**Nothing falls through.** Every follow-up has an owner and a due date, and
overdue work is visible to management on the dashboard. The commitments that
quietly disappear today become visible.

**Spend where it works.** Source performance shows which channel produces
customers who actually convert, not just customers who arrive.

**Customers are treated properly.** A NO is respected exactly once, then
honoured. This protects your reputation, and it protects your WhatsApp number —
providers restrict senders that generate complaints.

**Nothing depends on one person's memory.** If a staff member leaves tomorrow,
their customers, conversations, commitments and history are all still there, and
someone else can pick up mid-conversation with full context.

**Every claim is checkable.** The activity log means "who changed this and when"
always has an answer.

---

## 12. What happens next

1. Decide the WhatsApp provider and the follow-up waiting time — these two
   unblock everything else.
2. Connect the provider and turn on the scheduler.
3. Enter the remaining decisions in Settings.
4. Build the automated safety net before real customers are on the system.
5. Test on a small group of real customers before switching everyone on.
6. Go live.

Steps 1 and 2 are the difference between a system that works and a system that
sits still. Everything else can improve after launch.
